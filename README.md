# KassayCheck

An independent, source-by-source review of the claims on the Friends of Kassay for Assembly page **"Rebecca Delivers"** (<https://www.kassayforassembly.com/rebecca-delivers>), reviewed **October 24, 2026** (updated the same day with additional claim-level findings).

Rebecca Kassay (D) has represented New York State Assembly District 4 since January 1, 2025, and is running for re-election in November 2026 against William Sussman (R, C).

> Scope: this repo tests whether each claim is **verifiable** and **accurate as stated**. It does not rate the candidate, her opponent, or her policy positions.

## Bottom line

The page is **largely grounded in real, checkable events**, and none of the claims tested were found to be false. The two legislative wins it leads with are fully verified and signed into law. The problems are mostly **framing and precision**, not fabrication:

1. **Credit inflation on big numbers.** The $6.5M Mill Pond Dam figure is the *total* state commitment; $2.75M was already committed by the Governor before Kassay's budget add of $3.75M. Total formula school aid (e.g., $282.7M for Longwood) is presented under "Rebecca Delivers" though it flows by statewide formula, not by a single member's action.
2. **One legislative claim overreaches.** The bills Kassay *introduced* on cannabis govern **licensed** retailers and local zoning input. The bill that targets **illicit** cannabis (A10698) was sponsored by another member; Kassay was a co-sponsor.
3. **One volunteer number is higher than her own office reported.** The page says "over 100 smoke detectors"; her office's release says "nearly 100" in 74 homes.
4. **Omitted outcome.** The Port Jefferson–Rocky Point football advocacy is listed without noting the merger was denied and Port Jefferson fielded no varsity team in 2026.
5. **Most small grants have no public paper trail.** 19 grant line items ($10K–$180K) could not be confirmed or refuted from public sources. That is common for legislative-initiative grants, but it means they rest on the campaign's word. Where a paper trail does exist, it sometimes shows less than the page claims: Port Jefferson schools ($50K found vs. $130K claimed) and Comsewogue ($48K found vs. $96K claimed).

## Page-level issues

- **Repetition reads as volume.** Shared grants (Three Village $150K, Setauket FD $100K, TVHS $180K, Greenway law, etc.) are repeated under up to six community tabs. Each tab is accurate on its own, but anyone tallying the page will double-count.
- **Inconsistent descriptions of the same grant.** The $130K Port Jefferson schools grant is "STEM and environmental education" in one tab and "STEM and student enrichment" in another. The Three Village $150K is "summer reading" in four tabs and generic "enrichment" in another.
- **Possibly overlapping SCPD grants.** "$20,000 for SCPD e-bike enforcement" (Port Jeff / Belle Terre) and "$40,000 for SCPD Behavioral Health Unit and e-bike enforcement" (Port Jeff Station / Terryville / Three Village) may describe the same money. The page doesn't say.
- **Typo:** "CRP" should be "CPR" (Three Village tab).
- **No sources or dates.** None of the ~60 claims link to a bill, release, or budget document, so readers can't check them.
